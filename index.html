Our story

“We don't make bread to be fast. We make it to be fed — to the starter, to the fire, to whoever's waiting at the door.” — Marisol Ortiz, founder

Hearth & Grain opened in the old Pearl Street icehouse in 1994, when Marisol Ortiz took over the empty cold-storage building and installed a single wood-fired deck oven where the ice used to sit. The brick walls, built to hold a temperature, turned out to hold heat just as well.

The bakery has kept the same rhythm ever since: one starter, fed twice daily since the first batch; one loaf baked each day of the week, never two; ovens lit before dawn so the first bread is ready by the time the block wakes up. Nothing here is made ahead and held — what's on the shelf is what came out that morning.

Marisol still opens most mornings. Her daughter Teo runs the pastry bench now, and the two of them argue, amiably, about whether the babka counts as bread or dessert. It's on the schedule as bread. Teo has not conceded the point.
